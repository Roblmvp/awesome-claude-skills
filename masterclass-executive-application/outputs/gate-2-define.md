## First-Principles Analysis — Story 1

- **What was Rob really trying to understand?** How good acquisition decisions actually happen — and whether the judgment inside them could be made visible, connected, and eventually autonomous.
- **What assumption did he challenge?** That decision quality is a talent-and-experience problem. He came to see it as an information-architecture problem: the signals exist, but they live in systems that don't speak.
- **What hidden system became visible?** The acquisition decision loop — days supply, historical sales, stocking needs by class, price points, recon capacity/time-to-line, merchandising, vendor performance, lead conversion, staffing — synthesized in managers' heads because no system connects them.
- **How did his motivation change?** From consuming reports → to wiring workflows together → to founding Vyaxis and pushing PaceIQ to beta → to hitting a wall where the work outgrew his technical depth, patience, and time. The wall didn't end the motivation; it redefined it as a mandate to grow.
- **Why did it matter beyond the task?** "It will exist whether I create it or someone else." Inevitability — he intends to be a builder of his industry's future, not a bystander to it.
- **What does this reveal about how Rob learns?** By investigating, wiring, and shipping — not by waiting for permission or curriculum. (Exactly the learner the program's self-paced, project-based design assumes.)
- **What does this reveal about how Rob leads?** He upgrades the system everyone decides inside, and he is honest about his own limits — mature, not performative.
