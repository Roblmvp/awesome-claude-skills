## 1. Executive summary

The U.S. auto retail economy in late July 2026 is running on a **narrow, credit-extended, top-heavy demand base that is holding — but holding on terms that compress dealer margins and punish balance-sheet sloppiness.** Sales pace is the best of the year (June SAAR 16.5M, July forecast 16.7M [E1]) yet *retail* sales fell 4.1% in H1 — the headline is fleet-flattered [E2]. Credit is the loosest since 2015 [C1] while subprime delinquency sits at 32-year records [C3] — lenders are stretching terms and financing record negative equity to keep payments possible, which sustains today's volume by borrowing against tomorrow's severity. The Fed under new Chair Kevin Warsh is hawkish and on hold at 3.50–3.75% with markets pricing **hikes, not cuts**, into 2027 [B5, B6]. The H1 Iran war pushed CPI to 4.2% before June's retreat to 3.5% [B1, A12]; oil remains the live swing variable.

**What is most likely (28%):** a grinding, K-shaped soft landing — no recession, inflation drifting toward 3%, Fed on extended hold, SAAR 15.5–16.3M, continued front-end gross compression carried by record fixed operations. **But the downside cluster is collectively larger:** a 25% chance of a conventional recession inside 24 months, a 20% chance of a sticky-inflation/hawkish-Fed regime (possible hikes, ~8% auto APRs, sub-15M SAAR with prices still rising), and 19% for a deepening rolling slowdown. Upside reacceleration: 8%. Separately, a **~25% supply-shock overlay** (Canada 50% tariffs effective Aug 19, USMCA wind-down, rare-earth/chip truce expiring ~Nov) can land on any scenario and would produce 2021-style scarcity: lower volume, *higher* gross.

**What dealers should do now:** treat liquidity and inventory aging as the survival variables (history says dealer failure is a liquidity event, not a P&L event); expand the lender bench before it's needed; ride the fixed-ops and used-scarcity moats; keep buying discipline on 2–4-year-old units into the rebuilding off-lease wave; and manage the next 90 days around a dense catalyst calendar (FOMC Jul 29, Q2 GDP Jul 30, Canada tariffs Aug 19, truce expiry ~Nov 1, midterms Nov 3).

## 2. What changed recently (the six months your last plan didn't include)

1. **SCOTUS struck down the IEEPA tariffs (Feb 20, 6-3)** — but the auto sector's core burden, Section 232's 25% on vehicles/parts, survived untouched. A 10% bridge surcharge expired July 24; replacement Section 301/338 duties are layering in, including **50% on Canadian goods effective Aug 19** [F1–F3]. ~$175B of refunds flow to importers — not consumers [F4].
2. **USMCA is now in a 10-year wind-down** — the administration declined to extend on July 1. U.S.–Mexico bilateral talks continue; Canada is excluded [F5]. This is the largest structural threat to North American vehicle and parts costs.
3. **A U.S.–Iran war (Feb–Jun) closed the Strait of Hormuz**, spiked Brent to ~$119 and CPI to 4.2%, then partially unwound; the June 17 ceasefire was breached July 7–8, Brent topped $90, then crashed 9% on July 27 when strikes were halted [A12]. Gas is $4.11, +31% y/y [E15]. Venezuela reopened (~1M b/d ramping) as a medium-term supply offset [F11].
4. **Kevin Warsh replaced Powell as Fed Chair (May 22)** — and proved hawkish: forward guidance scrapped, 2026 inflation projection raised, June SEP median implying one **hike** by year-end; futures price ~4.3% by mid-2027 [B4–B6].
5. **The student-loan default wave hit consumer credit:** ~3.6M defaults reported to bureaus in two quarters, average score damage −91 points, wage garnishment running since January — a credit-score supply shock aimed at the marginal auto buyer [C8].
6. **The EV market reset completed:** post-credit share stabilized near 5.8% (from 10.6% peak), hybrids boomed to ~14%, and the gas spike sent **used-EV values up 12–14% y/y** [E9]. Federal EV/emissions policy rolled back; California is building a parallel regime [F9].

## 3. Current economic regime

**Classification: late-cycle, low-churn, K-shaped expansion under a fading supply shock — "stagflation-lite, easing."** Growth is positive but slow (Q1 +2.1%, Q2 nowcast 1.6% [A1, A2]). The labor market is frozen rather than failing: firing is at 57-year lows (claims 187K) while hiring has stalled (+57K June payrolls, participation at a 5-year low) [A3–A5]. Inflation is an energy story — core CPI is 2.6% and near-tame; headline 3.5% off a 4.2% May peak; the Fed's preferred PCE gauge (4.1% May) is the number keeping Warsh hawkish [B1, B2]. Consumption is historically concentrated: the top 10% of earners ≈ 49% of spending, underwritten by an AI-concentrated equity market [D1, D9]. Dimension states (six-state scale): growth **stable (weak-side)**; inflation **moderate deterioration, peaking**; employment **moderate deterioration (hiring side)**; credit **improving access / deteriorating quality**; liquidity **stable**; consumer health **conflicting (K-shaped)**; financial conditions **stable-tightening**.

## 4. Current automotive retail regime

**Classification: resilient normalization under cost pressure.** Volume is stabilizing at a 16M-ish SAAR but is fleet-flattered (retail −4.1% H1) [E1, E2]. Pricing discipline is intact — ATP $49,758 (+0.6%), incentives pinned at 7.0% of ATP for 13 months — because the tariff cost (~$30B industry-wide) is being **absorbed** in the chain: MY25→26 stickers rose 10.4%, consumers paid +5.9%, OEMs and dealers ate ~4.5 points [E4]. Inventory is balanced in aggregate (80 days) and wildly bifurcated by brand (Toyota 37 vs Jeep 160) [E5]. Used is the strong side: values +2% y/y and orderly, cheap cars scarcest (sub-$15K at 33 days' supply) [E6, E7]. Dealer P&L: front-end GPU compression continuing (−4% to −7% y/y at the publics in Q1), F&I resilient, **fixed ops at records and carrying the store**, floorplan interest declining [E10]. Consolidation is at record pace; franchise value has bifurcated (VW blue-sky → ~$0; Buick-GMC re-rated up) [E11]. Affordability is the structural constraint: record payment ($777–813/mo), record amount financed ($44,156), record 84-month share (23.9%), 20.3% of buyers above $1,000/mo, down payments at a 6-year low, and the sub-$30K new market reduced to 15% of sales [E14, E16]. Dimension states: auto demand **stable (retail weak-side)**; affordability **severe deterioration (level), stable (direction)**; vehicle supply **stable, bifurcated**; dealer profitability **moderate deterioration, cushioned by fixed ops**.

## 5. Recession and slowdown probability

- **Conventional recession within 24 months: 25%.** Below the ~30% unconditional base rate for a 24-month window because near-term signals are unusually benign (record-low claims, 2-year-high job openings, positively sloped curve ~100bp, both ISMs expanding [A5, A6, A9, A14]) — but near base rate for months 12–24 because the policy stack (Canada tariffs, USMCA wind-down, truce expiry, market-priced hikes) loads risk into the back half. Note the horizon mismatch: prediction markets' ~10–12% is for calendar 2026 only [A13]; our 25% covers through July 2028.
- **Slowdown short of recession (rolling slowdown): 19%** — growth stalls near zero with occasional negative payroll months, but no NBER event.
- **Sticky-inflation / hawkish-Fed regime: 20%** — see §6; this includes the path the futures market currently prices.
- Combined odds of a materially adverse regime (B+C+D): **64%**. Combined benign (A+E): **36%**. So what: the modal single outcome is benign, but the weight of evidence says plan against margin compression and build the option to play offense — not the reverse.

## 6. Interest-rate outlook

The Fed holds at 3.50–3.75% (July 29 decision imminent at publication; ~62% hold / 38% hike priced on July 24 [B6]). The honest answer on direction is that **experts and markets disagree**: 78% of surveyed economists see no change through 2026 and a desk-survey median puts the first *cut* in Q2 2027, while the futures curve prices ~one hike by late 2026 and ~4.3% by mid-2027 [B6]. ORACLE's base case: **extended hold through mid-2027, cuts optional late-window; one hike is a live risk that defines Scenario D (20%).** Long rates offer little relief either way: 10-year ~4.7%, mortgage ~6.6% [B7, A14]. **Dealer translation:** auto APRs have barely followed the Fed down (~100bp of 2025 cuts → ~0.2pp on new-car APR; realized new-deal APR ~7.0% [B8]) and will not deliver H2 2026 relief. Do not build a plan that needs cheaper money. The payment engine (Annex D) shows ±100bp moves the average new payment only ~$21/month — **rates cannot fix affordability; only price, mix, term, and subvention can.** Watch: FOMC statements, 2-yr yield >4.75% (warning), any 2026 hike (Scenario D confirmation).

## 7. Bank and lender tightening outlook

Auto credit is the **loosest in a decade and still easing** — Dealertrack CAI at a 10-year high, approvals 73.8%, captives and banks leading, ABS funding abundant [C1, C2, C9]. But the easing is structural, not price: record >72-month share (31.1%), shrinking down payments, 56.7% of new loans starting underwater [C1, C5]. The red team's framing is adopted: **this is late-cycle reach-for-volume — credit is currently the floor under demand, and it will become the accelerant if it turns.** Q2 lender earnings were risk-on (Ally originations +21% with improving losses; Capital One +18.9% with deliberate subprime expansion and a reserve release) [C7]; lenders trimmed subprime share for three straight months mid-year — a recalibration, not a retreat [C11]. **Tightening probability by horizon (judgment):** next 6 months — low (~20%) absent a shock; 6–12 months — moderate (~35%) as student-loan damage and severity data accumulate; 12–24 months — meaningful (~45–55%) in Scenarios B/C/D. Most vulnerable segments: subprime/deep-subprime and recent-default student borrowers; most resilient: prime and super-prime (0.43% ABS delinquency [C3]). Likely first-mover changes when it comes: LTV caps and cash-down requirements at near-prime, then advance-rate cuts at subprime finance companies. **Dealer action:** expand the lender bench NOW — adding lenders takes 60–90 days and is free optionality; the store-level tell (approval-rate and stip trends in your F&I office) leads the national data by a quarter.

## 8. Consumer financial-health outlook

Two consumers, one average. The top two quintiles — equity-rich, HELOC-tapping ($446B, 16 straight quarterly increases [D8]), income-secure — are doing the buying (63% of new-vehicle buyers earn $100K+ [E16-adjacent, D10]). The bottom half is exhausted: 3.0% savings rate, >13% of card balances 90+ days delinquent (15-year high), revolving credit contracting at −4.7%, and a student-loan default wave that knocked an average 91 points off ~3.6M credit scores with garnishment running [D2–D4, C8]. Aggregate flows are *not* deteriorating (early-stage delinquency transitions improving [C4, D4]) — the stress is deep, not spreading. Sentiment is depressed but bouncing on gas relief (UMich 54.4, durables buying conditions +20% m/m [D5]) — durable only if oil stays down. **Forecast:** consumers can sustain vehicle demand near current levels 6–12 months; the 12–24-month path depends on three supports — labor freeze holding, equity market intact, oil contained — of which the equity market is the single point of failure for the top-decile spending that carries the market [D9]. A possible second student-loan default wave this fall would further shrink the financeable pool [C8]. **Dealer action:** court the trade-cycle of the affluent (they still transact); build sub-prime alternatives and co-signer processes for everyone else; track your market's cash-down-offered trend as the earliest local exhaustion signal.

## 9. Labor-market outlook

The low-hire/low-fire freeze is holding but thinning: record-low firing (claims 187K), stalled hiring (+92K/mo YTD average, participation 61.5%), quits stuck at 1.9% [A3–A6]. The AI-displacement wave is real and concentrated — tech announced cuts +83% y/y with AI the leading stated reason; job growth is concentrated in below-average-wage industries [A7, A8]. **Forecast:** base case, the freeze persists through mid-2027 with unemployment drifting 4.2→4.6%; the key risk is that frozen markets break nonlinearly — when firing starts from a no-hiring base, unemployment rises fast (Scenario B/C trigger). **Deterioration probability:** ~30% within 12 months, ~45% within 24. **Leading warnings:** claims 4-wk avg >260K, negative payroll revisions accumulating, Challenger >100K/mo for two months, continuing claims >2.0M. **Dealer translation:** each ~1pp rise in unemployment historically costs roughly 10%+ of retail SAAR; white-collar-heavy metros crack first this cycle — check your zip-code exposure. Watch weekly claims like you watch your DOC.

## 10. New-vehicle outlook

Base case: **SAAR 15.5–16.3M through 2027** (Cox FY26 forecast 15.8M [E3]), with retail softer than headline and fleet/mix doing the flattering. Prices grind up +4–8% by year-end as MY27 reprices the tariff cost [E4] — a Q4-2026 demand air pocket after pull-forward is a live risk (red-team point, adopted). Incentive discipline (7.0% for 13 months) is the keystone: if it breaks (>8.5% and climbing), the pricing regime has flipped and front-end gross goes with it. Brand dispersion is extreme — lean-inventory brands (Toyota 37 days) hold pricing power; fat brands (Jeep 160, Stellantis stable-but-heavy) buy share with cash [E5]. EV share stabilizes ~6–8% without federal support; hybrids are the growth mix (+9% forecast, heading toward ~16% share) [E9]. The entry market does not come back in-window: sub-$30K stays ~15% of sales; affordability-priced demand keeps leaking to used [E16]. **So what:** order to turn, not to share targets; weight hybrid allocation aggressively; treat any OEM pitch that assumes 17M+ retail SAAR as fantasy. **Trigger to reassess:** retail/fleet split showing retail growth for two consecutive months (upside); incentives >8.5% (downside).

## 11. Used-vehicle outlook

The red team flipped this call from "values fade through 2027" to **two-sided, skewed neutral-to-firm through mid-2027** — adopted. Supports: total used supply historically tight even as off-lease rebuilds (2.4M 2026 returns is still ~40% below the ~4M pre-2020 norm), sub-$15K scarcity acute (33 days [E6]), new-vehicle price increases (+4–8%) pushing substitution *into* used, ~1M priced-out new buyers, a 13-year-old fleet [E8, D10]. Caps: off-lease returns +25.7% in 2026 and ~2.8M in 2027, 3-yr residuals at a 5-year low (66% of MSRP), EV lease returns +230% pressuring 2–4-year-old EV values [E8]. Base case: Manheim ends 2026 ~+2% (Cox call [E7]), stays in a ±3% band through 2027; the post-2027 pipeline thins again (weak 2025–26 lease originations) — **a genuine used-scarcity premium re-emerges in 2028.** Watch: auction conversion (<50% = demand cracking; it leads the index), MUVVI y/y >+4% (substitution winning — used gross upside), 3-yr residuals <64% (supply winning). **So what:** the used department is the margin refuge while new compresses — but buy to a 45-day clock, price to market weekly, and treat 2–4-year-old EVs as turn merchandise, not appreciation bets.

## 12. Affordability outlook

The binding constraint of the cycle, and it does not resolve in-window. Records across the board: $777–813 average new payment, $44,156 financed, 23.9% of loans at 84+ months, 20.3% of buyers above $1,000/mo, down payments at 11.6% of price (6-year low), 29.6% of trades underwater at a record −$6,884 average [E14, C5]. Structurally better than a year ago on income growth (VAI 35.3 weeks, −3.4% y/y) but at painful absolute levels [E14]. Ownership costs went the wrong way in H1: fuel +31% y/y (~+$48/mo for a typical driver) swamps the lone relief line, insurance at −4.1% CPI (~−$8/mo) [E15]. The payment engine (Annex D) quantifies the policy-relevant asymmetries: ±100bp of APR ≈ ±$21/mo (rates can't fix it); a 0% subvention ≈ $8,000 price-equivalent (why captives target it); +1pt APR needs only a 2.7% price cut to neutralize (incentives can offset rate shocks); and each term extension 60→72→84 months cuts ~$110/mo while adding ~$1,700–1,800 of interest (why terms keep stretching). **Forecast:** affordability improves only slowly in A/E (income growth + flat prices), worsens outright in D (APRs ~8%, prices rising). **So what:** merchandise by payment band, not price; master the OBBBA interest-deduction talking point (~$400–800/yr real value on qualifying U.S.-assembled units [F7]); build the sub-$600/mo used pipeline as the volume anchor.

## 13. Inventory outlook

Aggregate days' supply stays manageable (75–90) in A/B; the story is dispersion and aging. Fat-brand dealers carry the industry's risk: Jeep/Ram/Dodge/Chrysler at 129–160 days versus Toyota/Lexus/Honda under 45 [E5]. Tariff repricing at MY27 changeover will test demand elasticity brand-by-brand; expect OEM production discipline to be uneven. The supply-shock overlay (~25%: Canada 50% duties, USMCA escalation, chip/rare-earth truce lapse ~Nov [F3, F5, F12]) would flip specific brands from fat to scarce within weeks — 2021 mechanics on a smaller scale. Used acquisition stays hardest at the bottom (sub-$15K, 33 days) — the service lane and street-buy programs are the moat [E6]. **So what:** grade every stocking decision against your brand's days' supply, not the industry's; pre-negotiate floorplan assistance if you carry a fat brand; keep a 15–20% inventory dry-powder reserve to exploit dislocations (auction panic weeks, competitor liquidations). **Triggers:** own-brand >100 days (defense); a supply-shock event on a brand you stock (offense — buy forward immediately).

## 14. Wholesale-value outlook

Orderly is the word: Manheim 211.5 mid-July, +2.0% y/y, depreciation slightly above long-term average, conversion healthy at 55–57% [E7]. Base case: ±3% band through 2027 (see §11 dynamics). The tail risks are asymmetric by scenario: in C (recession), wholesale first *firms* (repo supply is bought by demand shifting down-market; 2008-09 analog: used values recovered before new) then softens; in D, nominal used prices rise with inflation while volumes fall; in the supply-shock overlay, wholesale spikes. Repo volume (highest since 2009 and rising [C6]) adds supply but concentrated in older, rougher units. **So what:** wholesale dislocation weeks — an auction panic, a lender liquidation — are buying opportunities for fortress-balance-sheet stores; conversion rate is your earliest tell, watch it weekly.

## 15. Dealer-profitability outlook

The glide path continues: front-end GPU normalization (not collapse) — new GPUs −4% to −7% y/y at the publics, used compressing more slowly, F&I resilient (~$1,850–1,900 PVR at publics), SG&A creeping up, floorplan interest falling [E10]. The correct mental baseline is 2019 economics plus scale — not 2021–22 windfall. Base case: total store gross roughly flat to −5% annually through 2027, with mix shifting further toward fixed ops and used; net margins compress ~30–60bp at stores that don't reset expenses. Scenario D is the P&L wildcard: volume down but gross UP if supply shocks land (2021 mechanics). Watch ABG/LAD/GPI/AN Q2 prints (this week) for the glide-path slope [E10]; a new-GPU decline >15% y/y at the publics = incentive war underway. Franchise-value bifurcation continues — record buy-sell volume, luxury commanding premiums, weak franchises de-rating toward zero (VW at $0–5M blue-sky) [E11]; Carvana's new-car pilot is small but strategically loud [E12]. **So what:** run the store to a 2019-plus-inflation expense model; defend F&I product penetration (rate markup is the vulnerable half); if you hold a weak franchise, the bid may be better now than later.

## 16. Fixed-operations outlook

The most durable profit stream in every scenario — including recession. Drivers: ~13-year-old fleet [D10], tariff-inflated parts prices lifting RO values [E4], recall waves feeding bays (Ford ~950K vehicles [E13]), record service revenue at the publics [E10]. NADA: service/parts = 13.3% of dealership revenue and typically >50% of profit [E13]. Risks: dealers have ceded 12% of service visits to independents since 2018; 45% of owners report cost-surprise dissatisfaction; technician scarcity persists [E13]. In a downturn, customer-pay defers — but deferred work becomes a backlog asset if you track declined services and recapture systematically. **So what:** fixed absorption above 85% is the recession insurance policy; invest here first (techs, capacity, retention marketing) before any facility or acquisition spend; build the declined-work recapture list now.

## 24. What could make this forecast wrong (red-team findings, retained)

1. **The benign case may be under-weighted.** Every major 2025–26 headwind is in visible retreat (war de-escalating, tariff regime shrinking post-SCOTUS, June CPI falling m/m, claims at 1969 lows, JOLTS at 2-year highs). If the participation drop is demographic (lower break-even payrolls), +57K isn't stall speed. A symmetric read puts A+E at 40–50%.
2. **The Fed path fights the futures curve.** Our base case (hold, cuts optional 2027) contradicts market pricing (~4.3% by mid-2027). If the market is right, Scenario D is under-weighted at 20% and auto APRs go to ~8% without a recession.
3. **Used values could firm, not fade** — off-lease supply is still ~40% below pre-2020 norms while new prices rise +4–8%; substitution could push Manheim above +4% y/y, flipping the dealer P&L assumptions (better used gross and trade equity, shrinking negative-equity problem).
4. **"Loose credit as floor" may invert to accelerant** — Ally/COF growing originations into record subprime delinquency with reserve releases resembles 2006 underwriting behavior; when availability turns, it turns fast.
5. **July's strong SAAR may be borrowed demand** — fleet plus pull-forward ahead of Aug 19 tariffs and telegraphed price increases; watch the Aug–Sep retail/fleet split for the payback.
6. **Rebuild (don't re-weight) conditions:** two+ hikes by Dec 2026 with positive payrolls; Canada 50% implemented AND US-Mexico collapse; a Sahm-rule trigger while claims are still <220K (the freeze model broke); Nasdaq −25% with top-decile spending flat (wealth-transmission model wrong); core CPI >4% with Brent <$85 (inflation decoupled from oil); Dealertrack CAI −5 points in a quarter.

# Annex B — Historical analogs (ranked, with misleading aspects)

Method: analogs source *mechanisms*, not outcomes; each lists what transfers and what misleads. Final similarity scores set after evidence integration.

1. **2015–16 rolling industrial slowdown — 8/10.** Transfers: sector-by-sector weakness without aggregate recession; subprime auto stress rising while prime stays clean; SAAR plateau after a strong run; national averages masking deep sector pain. Misleads: the 2016 demand levers (cheap money, fresh term extension, low prices) are largely spent today — 84mo is already mainstream, ATP already record. Verdict: primary template for Scenario B and the base-case "grinding" texture.
2. **2022–24 rate/inflation cycle — 8/10 as initial condition, 3/10 as path.** It *is* the inherited state: record ATPs, negative-equity flows from 2021-22 peak-price cohorts (returning now at ~4 years old), GPU normalization glide, insurance shock. Misleads: extrapolating windfall economics as "normal" — the correct baseline is 2019-plus-inflation. Verdict: defines initial conditions.
3. **1990–91 oil-shock recession — 6/10 (raised from 5).** Transfers: geopolitical oil spike into a late-cycle economy with credit tightening; short, demand-led auto contraction then recovery. H1 2026 already ran the opening act (Hormuz, $119 Brent, sentiment crash). Misleads: 1990's S&L credit crunch has no clean analog; today's credit is loose, not tight. Verdict: template for C-via-oil.
4. **2001 capex-bust recession — 6/10.** Transfers: investment-bubble unwind (then IT, now AI) hitting white-collar employment first; consumer held up longer than feared. Misleads: 2001's consumer was cushioned by housing/refi — today's marginal buyer has no refi channel and the top-decile cushion is the *equity market itself*, i.e., the thing that would be falling; AI capex is cash-flow-funded (mega-cap), not debt/IPO-funded, so the trigger requires more than sentiment. Verdict: skeleton for C-via-AI-retrench, with the red team's funding caveat attached.
5. **1995–98 opportunistic-easing soft landing — 5/10 (cut from 7).** Transfers: mid-cycle policy adjustment with productivity narrative and wealth effects. Misleads: the 1990s Fed was *cutting* into contained inflation; Warsh is holding against 4% PCE with hike risk — the policy configuration is nearly opposite. Broad-based 1990s income gains vs today's K-shape. Verdict: keep only for Scenario A/E mechanics.
6. **1970s stagflation / early-80s tightening — 3/10.** Transfers: cost-push inflation with politically pressured monetary policy; durable-goods deferral cycles. Misleads: no wage-price spiral, expectations still ~3.3% not double digits, labor market frozen not overheated. Verdict: tail reference for D; the watch item is 5-yr expectations >3.5%.
7. **2008–09 GFC — 3/10, mechanism library only.** Transfers: negative equity as severity accelerant; lender-exit cascades; liquidity kills dealers (hundreds failed on floorplan calls). Misleads: subprime auto (~16% of originations, small ABS stack vs bank capital) lacks the leverage chain to be systemic; "2008 for cars" narratives (common in field anecdotes) overweight this analog. Verdict: severity mechanics and the liquidity lesson only.
8. **2020–22 pandemic/supply shock — 2/10 as path, but its supply-side lesson is live.** Constrained supply + solvent demand = record GPU. This is exactly the supply-shock overlay (~25%): if Canada-50%/USMCA/chip-truce shocks land on firm demand, expect low-volume/high-gross, not low-volume/low-gross. Verdict: the overlay playbook.

**Cross-analog law (drives the Resilience Index and the defensive sets):** dealer failure is a liquidity event, not a P&L event — stores die from floorplan calls, aged-inventory water, and lender concentration, not from one bad year of gross.

# Annex D — Payment model (methodology + outputs)

**Method (disclosed):** standard amortization, M = P·(r/12)/(1−(1+r/12)^−n). **Inputs (verified, Q2 2026):** new — $44,156 financed @ 7.0% / 70mo (Edmunds); used — $30,414 @ 10.8% / 68mo (Edmunds/Q1 APR); income $84,000 (Census-based approximation — assumption); insurance $2,158/yr (Insurify 2026 projection). **Validation:** computed new payment $770 vs Edmunds' reported $777 — within 1%.

- **Representative payments:** new $770/mo · used $600/mo.
- **Rate sensitivity (new):** −200bp → −$42/mo · −100bp → −$21 · +100bp → +$21 · +200bp → +$43. *Rates alone cannot fix a $770 payment.*
- **Payment-neutral price cuts:** +50bp needs −$600 (−1.4%) · +100bp −$1,189 (−2.7%) · +200bp −$2,334 (−5.3%). *Incentives can offset plausible rate shocks.*
- **Subvention value (payment-equivalent price cut at 7.0% market rate):** 0% APR ≈ $7,989 · 1.9% ≈ $5,919 · 3.9% ≈ $3,661.
- **Payment-to-income (with insurance):** median household 11.0% (13.6%) · $60K household 15.4% (19.0%) · $150K household 6.2% (7.6%). *The K-shaped market, quantified: the average new car fails the 10% affordability heuristic for the median household and clears it easily at $150K.*
- **Tier matrix (new note, 72mo):** prime-weighted 6.39% → $740 · all-credit 9.58% → $809 · subprime 13.44% → $897. Subprime used ($30.4K @ 19.42%/68mo) → ~$741 — the used subprime buyer pays nearly the prime new-car payment for a vehicle at 69% of the price.
- **Term-extension math (new @7.0%):** 60mo $874 (+$8.3K interest) · 72mo $753 (+$10.0K) · 84mo $666 (+$11.8K) — each step "saves" ~$90–120/mo at ~$1,700–1,800 more interest and slower equity build; this is the machine that manufactures the next negative-equity cohort.
